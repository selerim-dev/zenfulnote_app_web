Journaling can become another place where the inner critic performs.

You open the page hoping to understand yourself, but within a few lines you are building a case against yourself. You should have known better. You should be further along. You should not still feel this.

That kind of writing may be honest, but it is not always useful.

## Begin with what happened

Start with the observable details. What was said? What did you hear? What did your body do? What did you want to do next?

This helps separate the moment from the story forming around it.

"I got quiet after the comment."

"I checked my phone three times instead of answering."

"I felt embarrassed and wanted to leave."

Facts create enough ground for reflection.

## Ask what the moment touched

Once the facts are visible, ask a deeper question: what did this touch in me?

Maybe it touched shame. Maybe it touched grief. Maybe it touched a fear of being misunderstood. Maybe it touched a desire you have not admitted yet.

You do not need to force an answer. The question itself softens the habit of self-attack.

## Let compassion be accurate

Self-compassion is not pretending the hard thing did not happen. It is refusing to turn pain into proof that you are wrong for being human.

<Callout>
  Honest writing and gentle writing can happen on the same page.
</Callout>

End with one next step that does not punish you. Rest. Repair. Ask for clarity. Return later. Let the page become a place where awareness can breathe.
