A good prompt does not demand a performance. It opens a door.

Sometimes the answer arrives quickly. Sometimes the first answer is surface-level. Sometimes the most honest response is, "I do not know yet."

All three are useful.

## Read the question slowly

Before writing, read the prompt once and notice your first reaction.

Do you feel curious? Resistant? Tender? Annoyed? Blank?

That reaction is already information. It may show you where the question is touching something alive.

## Follow the first honest line

You do not need to answer the entire question perfectly. Start with the first honest line and let it lead.

"I do not want to write about this because..."

"The first thing that comes up is..."

"I think I have been avoiding..."

The entry can unfold from there.

## Stop before you over-explain

Reflection becomes more powerful when you leave room for the next layer to appear later.

<Callout>
  A prompt is not asking you to solve yourself. It is inviting you to listen more closely.
</Callout>

If the page gives you one clear sentence, that may be enough for today.
