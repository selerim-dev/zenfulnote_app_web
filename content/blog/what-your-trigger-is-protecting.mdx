A trigger can feel like an interruption, but it is often a form of protection.

Something in you reacts quickly because it believes the moment matters. It may not be responding only to what is happening now. It may be responding to what the present moment resembles.

## Protection can look like intensity

You might become defensive, withdrawn, agreeable, impatient, or suddenly certain that you need to leave.

These responses are not random. They often formed around earlier moments when you needed a strategy. The problem is not that the strategy exists. The problem is when the strategy takes over before you can choose.

## Curiosity creates space

Instead of asking, "Why am I like this?" try asking, "What is this part of me trying to prevent?"

Maybe it is trying to prevent rejection. Maybe it is trying to prevent embarrassment. Maybe it is trying to prevent disappointment from feeling familiar again.

This does not excuse harmful behavior. It helps you understand what is underneath it.

<Callout>
  Shadow work begins when you can look at a reaction without immediately becoming it.
</Callout>

## Integration starts small

The next step may be one sentence in your journal: "A part of me felt unsafe when..."

That sentence can create enough room to respond differently next time.
