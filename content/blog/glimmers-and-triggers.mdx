Triggers are easy to notice because they interrupt you. They arrive with heat, urgency, contraction, or the sudden need to defend yourself.

Glimmers are quieter. They can be a song, a clean room, a soft exchange, sunlight through a window, or the brief sense that your body is safe enough to exhale.

Both matter.

## Triggers show where protection begins

A trigger is not proof that you are broken. It is a signal that some part of you is trying to protect something.

Sometimes it is protecting a boundary. Sometimes it is protecting an old wound. Sometimes it is protecting a version of you that learned to stay alert because being relaxed did not feel safe.

When you track a trigger, you create distance from the reaction. You can ask: what did this touch in me?

## Glimmers show where safety returns

If triggers show where protection begins, glimmers show where safety returns.

They teach you what steadiness feels like. They remind your nervous system that not every moment has to be scanned for danger. They also show you what your life may need more of.

A glimmer is not small just because it is quiet.

<Callout>
  A fuller pattern appears when you track both what activates you and what helps you come back.
</Callout>

## The goal is range

You are not trying to become someone who never reacts. You are building more range around the reaction.

That range might look like pausing before responding. Naming the feeling before spiraling. Noticing when a conversation drains you. Letting beauty register for longer than one second.

Your inner life becomes clearer when you stop tracking only what hurts.
